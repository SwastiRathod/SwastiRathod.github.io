idle questions
What if...?
Small speculative questions, answered in a few sentences — the kind that come up mid-shift or mid-problem-set.
What if the Higgs field switched off?
Every fundamental particle that gets its mass from it — electrons, quarks — would instantly become massless. Atoms couldn't hold together, and chemistry as we know it would stop existing within a fraction of a second.
What if protons could decay?
Grand unified theories predict it should happen, but experiments like Super-Kamiokande have only set a lower limit of over 10³⁴ years — far longer than the age of the universe. If confirmed, it would mean all matter is temporary.
What if there were no strong force?
Quarks would never bind into protons and neutrons, so no atomic nuclei — and nothing heavier than a lone electron and a free-floating quark — could ever form.
What if space had one extra dimension?
Gravity would weaken faster with distance, and stable orbits like Earth's around the Sun likely couldn't exist. It's part of why three spatial dimensions may not be an accident.
What if neutrinos truly had zero mass?
That was the original Standard Model assumption. But neutrino oscillation experiments proved otherwise — flavour-changing behaviour is only possible if neutrinos carry at least a tiny mass.
